\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
Apart from \cref{sec:real}, all quantitative results come from simulation. LunarSim reproduces the statistical character of
lunar relief and the dominant photometric effects, but it omits near-field rocks below the $0.3$\,m DEM spacing (\cref{fig:apollo}), dust, lens
effects and most geological units. Absolute recall values are not predictions of on-Moon
performance, and the real-image tests are small, with station-level ground truth. Finally, the queries are upright and near the database poses. A $5^\circ$ roll
lowers the frozen reference head from $\rbBase$ to $\rbRollFive\%$ R@1 and a $30^\circ$ heading change costs
$\rbHeadThirtyDrop$ points (supplement); levelling the image with gravity recovers most of the roll loss.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ======================================================================
With both stages of lunar VPR cast as optimal transport, illumination-diverse training, residual
aggregation with a fixed assignment and global--local fusion matter most in simulation. On real
Apollo photographs only fusion carries over. Next steps are larger real benchmarks at the Apollo~15--17 sites, training on DESPINA renders
of real terrain~\cite{nelsonarcher2026despina}, and local evidence that survives Sun changes, which neither keypoints nor boulder
segments provided here.
