\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
All quantitative results come from simulation. LunarSim reproduces the statistical character of
lunar relief and the dominant photometric effects, but it omits several things: resolved near-field rocks below the $0.3$\,m DEM spacing (\cref{fig:apollo}), multiple
scattering beyond a constant ambient term, rover self-shadowing and dust, lens flare, exposure
bracketing, and the full range of geological units. Absolute recall values are therefore not predictions of on-Moon
performance. The \emph{relative} comparisons use identical conditions and paired queries on unseen
scenes, so we expect them to carry over better, but this must be confirmed on real imagery. Fine-tuning used a single learning rate selected on one
validation scene and four of twelve blocks; full fine-tuning or larger backbones were not explored. The FGW structural term uses image-plane
distances, which are only approximately preserved under large viewpoint changes. Lifting database
keypoints to 3-D with DEM depth is a natural extension.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ======================================================================
Galatea casts both stages of lunar place recognition as optimal transport: a semi-relaxed
unbalanced assignment that unifies NetVLAD and SALAD, and partial fused Gromov--Wasserstein
re-ranking fused with the global score. On LunarSim-VPR,
(i)~terrestrial models, including foundation-model ones, lose more than half their recall under
large Sun changes;
(ii)~illumination-diverse training matters far more than the aggregation layer; with a frozen
backbone balanced and unbalanced assignments perform alike, as the theory predicts, and joint
fine-tuning adds $11$--$19$ points for every head;
(iii)~fusing local with global evidence adds three to seven points of Recall@1 for every local
matcher. Next steps are real imagery, with DESPINA-synthesised reference views of real
sites~\cite{nelsonarcher2026despina}, and 3-D structural terms from DEM depth.
