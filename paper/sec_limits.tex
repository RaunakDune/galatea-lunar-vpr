\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
All quantitative results come from simulation. LunarSim reproduces the statistical character of
lunar relief and the dominant photometric effects, but it omits several things: resolved near-field rocks below the $0.3$\,m DEM spacing (\cref{fig:apollo}), multiple
scattering beyond a constant ambient term, rover self-shadowing and dust, lens flare, exposure
bracketing, and the full range of geological units. Absolute recall values are therefore not predictions of on-Moon
performance; the relative comparisons should carry over better, but this must be confirmed on real
imagery. Fine-tuning used a single learning rate selected on one
validation scene and four of twelve blocks; full fine-tuning or larger backbones were not explored,
and the ranking of heads under fine-tuning may depend on these choices. The test set has $450$
places in three scenes, each matched against the $150$ database images of its own scene. The FGW structural term uses image-plane
distances, which large viewpoint changes do not preserve. Finally, the queries are upright and near the database poses. In further tests (supplement), a $5^\circ$ roll
lowers the frozen reference head from $\rbBase$ to $\rbRollFive\%$ R@1, and fine-tuned models lose nearly as much; levelling the
image with gravity recovers most of this, while a $30^\circ$ heading change costs $\rbHeadThirtyDrop$ points.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ======================================================================
Galatea studies lunar visual place recognition by casting both stages as optimal transport: a family
of semi-relaxed unbalanced assignments that contains the NetVLAD and SALAD assignments, and a partial
fused Gromov--Wasserstein matcher whose evidence is fused with the global score by a rule with bounded
override. As the theory predicted, the members of the family are equivalent on a frozen backbone, and
the loss does not identify a trainable relaxation; once the backbone is fine-tuned, a trainable
$\lambda$ ends wherever it starts, and residual aggregation is best. The assignment should therefore be
a fixed design choice. Global--local fusion improved every stage-1 model and every local matcher we
tested, because it keeps about half of what local evidence rescues while rarely overturning a correct
global decision, and illumination-diverse training had a larger effect than backbone fine-tuning or any
choice of assignment. Next steps are real imagery, with DESPINA-synthesised reference
views of real sites~\cite{nelsonarcher2026despina}, and 3-D structural terms from DEM depth.
