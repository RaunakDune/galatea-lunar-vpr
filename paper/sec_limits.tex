\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
All quantitative results come from simulation. LunarSim reproduces the statistical character of
lunar relief and the dominant photometric effects, but it omits several things: resolved near-field rocks below the $0.3$\,m DEM spacing (\cref{fig:apollo}), multiple
scattering beyond a constant ambient term, rover self-shadowing and dust, lens flare, exposure
bracketing, and the full range of geological units. Absolute recall values are therefore not predictions of on-Moon
performance. The \emph{relative} comparisons use identical conditions and paired queries on unseen
scenes, so we expect them to carry over better, but this must be confirmed on real imagery. Fine-tuning used a single learning rate selected on one
validation scene and four of twelve blocks; full fine-tuning or larger backbones were not explored,
and the ranking of heads under fine-tuning may depend on these choices. The FGW structural term uses image-plane
distances, which are only approximately preserved under large viewpoint changes. Lifting database
keypoints to 3-D with DEM depth is a natural extension.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ======================================================================
Galatea treats lunar visual place recognition as optimal transport at both stages: a family of
semi-relaxed unbalanced assignments for global aggregation, which contains the NetVLAD and SALAD
assignments and a learned relaxation, and a partial fused Gromov--Wasserstein matcher whose evidence
is fused with the global score by a rule with bounded override. The theory of the assignment family
predicted, and LunarSim-VPR confirmed, that its members are equivalent on a frozen backbone and that a learned
relaxation would not move; once the backbone is fine-tuned the family spreads only slightly, learning
$\lambda$ brings no benefit over fixing it, and residual aggregation is best. Global--local
fusion improved every stage-1 model and every local matcher, because it keeps about half of what local
evidence rescues without overturning confident global decisions. Illumination-diverse training
remained the largest single factor. Next steps are real imagery, with DESPINA-synthesised reference
views of real sites~\cite{nelsonarcher2026despina}, and 3-D structural terms from DEM depth.
