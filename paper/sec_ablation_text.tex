\Cref{tab:ablation} varies one factor at a time. Single-seed differences below about two points
should be read against the seed standard deviation of roughly one point (\cref{tab:main}).

\paragraph*{Assignment relaxation}
Every value of $\lambda$ gives the same accuracy within seed noise. R@1 is $\lamZeroRone\%$ at
$\lambda=0$ (three seeds), $\galRone\%$ with learned $\lambda$, and $\salRone\%$ at $\lambda=1$. When
$\lambda$ is learned, it barely leaves its initialisation ($\bar\lambda=\galLam$). Even with a
$25\times$ larger learning rate on $\lambda$ and $z$ it only reaches $\galFastLam$, at unchanged
accuracy ($\galFastRone\%$). Both follow from \cref{prop:colinv}: after intra-normalisation $\lambda$ acts
only through the token weights $w_i$, a weak training signal. The cheaper $\lambda=0$ assignment (one
softmax, no Sinkhorn loop) is thus as good as balanced OT here.

\paragraph*{Dust-bin, iterations and clusters}
Removing the dust-bin does not hurt ($\noDustRone\%$ vs.\ $\galSzeroRone\%$, single seed), since the
near-black sky is cheap to ignore; real stray light may change this. Changing the number of Sinkhorn iterations ($L=1$: $\LoneRone\%$, $L=10$: $\LtenRone\%$) moves R@1
by less than seed noise, in line with the fast contraction of \cref{prop:contraction}. Halving the
clusters to $m=32$ ($4352$-D) costs nothing ($\mThirtytwoRone\%$). With $m=16$ ($2304$-D), R@1 drops to $\mSixteenRone\%$.

\paragraph*{Illumination-diverse training}
Re-rendering the training scenes with the \emph{same} poses but a single (database) Sun direction
has by far the largest effect: Galatea then reaches
only $\fixIllRone\%$ R@1, $\fixIllDrop$ points below illumination-diverse training, and SALAD drops
the same way ($\salFixIllRone\%$). The single-illumination model is slightly \emph{better} when the
query light matches its training light (C0: $\fixIllCzero\%$ vs.\ $\galSzeroCzero\%$), but on the
three hardest conditions it falls to $\fixIllHardVal\%$, against $\galSzeroHard\%$. Fine-tuning makes
this worse: with a single training Sun the fine-tuned Galatea reaches $\ftFixIllRone\%$ ($-\ftFixIllDrop$;
hard $\ftFixIllHard\%$ vs.\ $\ftGalSzeroHard\%$) and SALAD $\ftSalFixIllRone\%$, since a more flexible
model fits the training light more closely. An illumination sweep in the training data matters far
more than the aggregation layer.
