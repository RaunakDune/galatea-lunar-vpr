\begin{abstract}
Visual place recognition (VPR) on the Moon must cope with black shadows that move with the Sun,
statistically self-similar crater fields and strongly non-Lambertian regolith. We present
\emph{Galatea}, a framework that treats both stages of two-stage VPR as optimal transport (OT).
For global aggregation, a semi-relaxed unbalanced OT problem defines a one-parameter family of
token-to-cluster assignments with the NetVLAD ($\lambda=0$) and SALAD ($\lambda=1$) assignments as end
points. We prove that its Sinkhorn iterations converge linearly at rate $\lambda$, characterise the
dust-bin as a logistic gate, and show that after intra-normalisation the descriptor sees the transport
plan only through per-token weights. For re-ranking, a training-free partial fused
Gromov--Wasserstein matcher scores the appearance and layout of keypoints, and a saturating fusion rule
provably needs local evidence that grows with the global margin to overturn the global ranking. On LunarSim-VPR, a physically motivated benchmark that renders the same query poses
under seven Sun conditions, the strongest terrestrial model falls from $\zsSaladCzero\%$ to
$\zsSaladHard\%$ Recall@1 under large Sun changes. With a frozen backbone, all values of $\lambda$
perform alike (about $60\%$; zero-shot: $\zsSaladRone\%$), as the theory predicts. Fine-tuning adds
$11$--$19$ points; the OT family then spreads by about two points, a learned $\lambda$ stays at its
initial value, and residual NetVLAD aggregation pulls ahead ($\ftNvRone\%$). Fusion adds
$\msnvMnnRGain$--$\msMnnRGain$ points for every stage-1 model and matcher we tested, because it keeps
about half of the local rescues while breaking few correct global decisions; the full system reaches
$\msnvFgwRone\%$. In ablations of the Galatea head, illumination-diverse training is worth
$\fixIllDrop$--$\ftFixIllDrop$ points, more than backbone fine-tuning.
\end{abstract}
