\begin{abstract}
Visual place recognition (VPR) on the Moon has to cope with black shadows that move with the Sun,
statistically self-similar crater fields and strongly non-Lambertian regolith. We present
\emph{Galatea}, a two-stage VPR system built on optimal transport (OT). Stage~1 aggregates
foundation-model tokens with a semi-relaxed unbalanced OT assignment whose relaxation parameter
$\lambda$ recovers the NetVLAD ($\lambda=0$) and SALAD ($\lambda=1$) assignments exactly. We prove
that its truncated Sinkhorn iterations converge linearly at rate $\lambda$, characterise the
dust-bin as a logistic gate on cluster affinity, and show that after intra-normalisation the
descriptor depends on the transport plan only through per-token weights. Stage~2 re-ranks
candidates with a training-free partial fused Gromov--Wasserstein matching of keypoints, and a
saturating score fuses this local evidence with the global similarity. We also introduce
LunarSim-VPR, a physically motivated procedural benchmark in which the same query poses are
rendered under seven Sun conditions. Terrestrial state-of-the-art models fall from about
$\zsSaladCzero\%$ Recall@1 at fixed illumination to $\zsSaladHard\%$ on the hardest Sun changes.
Training only the OT aggregation head on illumination-diverse lunar scenes raises overall Recall@1
from $\zsSaladRone\%$ to $\galRone\%$, and jointly fine-tuning the last backbone blocks lifts every
head by a further $11$--$19$ points (best: $\ftNvRone\%$). Global--local fusion adds
$\msFgwGain\pm\msFgwGainsd$ points on top of the frozen model and $\ftFgwGain\pm\ftFgwGainsd$ on top of
the fine-tuned one (three seeds). With a frozen backbone, balanced and unbalanced assignments perform
alike, as the theory predicts. The dominant factor is illumination-diverse training: a single training
Sun direction costs $\fixIllDrop$ points frozen and $\ftFixIllDrop$ fine-tuned.
\end{abstract}
