\begin{abstract}
Visual place recognition (VPR) on the Moon must cope with black shadows that move with the Sun,
statistically self-similar crater fields and strongly non-Lambertian regolith. We ask which design
choices of modern two-stage VPR matter under these conditions, and answer with \emph{Galatea}, a
controlled study that casts both stages as optimal transport (OT). For aggregation, a semi-relaxed
unbalanced OT problem defines a one-parameter family of token-to-cluster assignments with the NetVLAD
($\lambda=0$) and SALAD ($\lambda=1$) assignments as end points. Its Sinkhorn iterations contract at
rate $\lambda$, its dust-bin acts as a logistic gate, and after intra-normalisation the descriptor sees
the transport plan only through per-token weights, which predicts that $\lambda$ should matter little.
For re-ranking, a training-free partial fused Gromov--Wasserstein matcher scores the appearance and
layout of keypoints, and a saturating fusion rule needs local evidence that grows with the global
margin to overturn the global ranking. On LunarSim-VPR, an open benchmark that renders the same query
poses under seven Sun conditions, the strongest terrestrial model falls from $\zsSaladCzero\%$ to
$\zsSaladHard\%$ Recall@1 under large Sun changes. With a frozen backbone every assignment performs
alike (about $60\%$; zero-shot: $\zsSaladRone\%$), as predicted, and the loss does not identify a
trainable $\lambda$: it ends where it starts, and with a $100\times$ learning rate drifts without
converging, at unchanged accuracy. Fine-tuning adds $11$--$19$ points; the assignment then changes
Recall@1 by at most about three points, and residual NetVLAD aggregation is best ($\ftNvRone\%$).
Fusion adds $\msnvMnnRGain$--$\msMnnRGain$ points for every stage-1 model and matcher tested, and the
full system reaches $\msnvFgwRone\%$. Illumination-diverse training is worth
$\fixIllDrop$--$\ftFixIllDrop$ points, more than fine-tuning. We therefore recommend a fixed
assignment, residual aggregation, illumination-diverse training and fusion with a cheap matcher.
\end{abstract}
