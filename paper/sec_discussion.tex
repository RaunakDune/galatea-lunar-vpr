\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% ======================================================================
On LunarSim-VPR, training on lunar imagery ($+\gainZsMean$ points over the best zero-shot model) and
cluster-based aggregation (over $20$ points above global pooling) are prerequisites; the points below assume
both.

\paragraph*{1) Training data}
Illumination diversity in training is worth $\fixIllDrop$ points of R@1 for the reference head with a
frozen backbone and $\ftFixIllDrop$ with a fine-tuned one, more than fine-tuning adds to the same head.
Fine-tuning should be combined with illumination-diverse training.

\paragraph*{2) Backbone adaptation}
Fine-tuning the last four blocks adds $11$--$19$ points for every head. On real panoramas this gain does
not appear (\cref{sec:real}).

\paragraph*{3) Aggregation}
Among the cluster-based heads the aggregation design has a larger effect than the assignment. Residual
NetVLAD aggregation differs from its closest OT relative by $\famFrNvVsNDMean$ points on frozen tokens and by
$\famFtNvVsNDMean$ once the backbone is trained (NetVLAD minus OT), and is the best fine-tuned head.

\paragraph*{4) Assignment}
Within the OT family all values of $\lambda$ are equivalent on a frozen backbone (P1, P2), the fixed members
stay within $\famFtLamRange$ points of each other after fine-tuning, and training cannot choose among them
(\cref{sec:family,sec:ft}). The cheapest member, $\lambda=0$, needs no Sinkhorn loop.

\paragraph*{5) Re-ranking}
Local evidence on the Moon is abundant but unreliable, because shadow edges dominate keypoint detections;
under fusion it must grow with the global margin to overturn a global decision (\cref{prop:override}), and
all four local scorers perform alike. The cheapest, MNN+MAGSAC++, is a
sensible default; FGW ($\approx\tFgwK$\,ms per query on a CPU) is worth using only where no learned matcher
can be trusted.

\paragraph*{6) Theory}
Predictions (P1)--(P3) held for fixed tokens with a dust-bin. The ordering of heads with trained tokens is an
empirical finding on simulated data; on real photographs (\cref{sec:real}) only the weakness of GeM is
confirmed.
