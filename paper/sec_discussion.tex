\section{Discussion: What Matters for Lunar VPR}\label{sec:discussion}
% ======================================================================
We rank the design decisions by their measured effect on LunarSim-VPR. Two choices are prerequisites and are assumed below: training on
lunar imagery ($+\gainZsMean$ points over the best zero-shot model) and cluster-based aggregation
(more than $20$ points over global pooling).

\paragraph*{Training data first}
Illumination diversity in training is worth $\fixIllDrop$ points of R@1 for the reference head with a
frozen backbone and $\ftFixIllDrop$ with a fine-tuned one, more than fine-tuning adds to the same head,
so illumination diversity and fine-tuning have to go together.

\paragraph*{Then backbone adaptation, then aggregation}
Fine-tuning the last four blocks adds $11$--$19$ points for every head. Among the cluster-based heads,
the aggregation design matters more than the assignment: residual NetVLAD aggregation differs from its
closest OT relative by $\famFrNvVsNDMean$ points on frozen tokens and by $\famFtNvVsNDMean$ once the
backbone is trained (NetVLAD minus OT). Within the OT family the assignment is a weak lever:
all values of $\lambda$ are equivalent on a frozen backbone (P1, P2), the fixed members stay within
$\famFtLamRange$ points of each other once it is fine-tuned, and training cannot choose among them
(\cref{sec:family,sec:ft}). The cheapest member, $\lambda=0$, needs no Sinkhorn loop and is the
assignment of residual NetVLAD aggregation, the best fine-tuned head.

\paragraph*{Fusion, not the matcher}
Local evidence on the Moon is abundant but unreliable, because shadow edges dominate keypoint
detections; under the fusion rule it must grow with the global margin to overturn a global decision
(\cref{prop:override}). With fusion, all four local scorers perform alike, so the cheapest,
MNN+MAGSAC++, is a sensible default; FGW ($\approx\tFgwK$\,ms per query on a CPU) is worth using only
where no learned matcher can be trusted.

\paragraph*{What the theory did and did not predict}
Predictions (P1)--(P3) held for fixed tokens with a dust-bin. The ordering of heads with trained tokens is an empirical finding on simulated data; on real photographs
(\cref{sec:real}) only the weakness of GeM is confirmed.
