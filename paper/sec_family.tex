\subsection{The assignment family with a frozen backbone}\label{sec:family}\label{sec:stage1}
Training only the aggregation head on illumination-diverse LunarSim scenes lifts R@1 from
$\zsSaladRone\%$ to $\galRone\%$ (Galatea head, three seeds; paired gain $\gainZsMean$ points, 95\% CI
$[\gainZsLo,\gainZsHi]$) and mean R@1 over the three hardest conditions (C2, C5, C6) from
$\zsSaladHard\%$ to $\galHard\%$ (\cref{tab:main}). All cluster-based heads clearly beat every
zero-shot model, whereas GeM pooling of the same frozen tokens does not ($\gemRone\%$), presumably
because global pooling cannot separate place-specific from illumination-dependent
tokens~\cite{izquierdo2024optimal}.

\Cref{tab:family} (left) confirms predictions (P1) and (P2). With a dust-bin, every value of $\lambda$
from $0$ to $1$ reaches the same R@1 within seed noise (spread across fixed $\lambda$:
$\famFrLamRange$ points), and Galatea's learned head is statistically indistinguishable from SALAD
($\famFrGalVsSalMean$ points, CI $[\famFrGalVsSalLo,\famFrGalVsSalHi]$). A learned $\lambda$ barely leaves its
initialisation ($\bar\lambda=\galLam$); even with a $25\times$ larger learning rate on $\lambda$ and
$z$ it only reaches $\galFastLam$, at unchanged accuracy ($\galFastRone\%$). This happens although the
heads spend mass very differently: the balanced head sends $\salDust\%$ of the token mass to the
dust-bin, an amount that tracks the sky fraction of the image ($\rho=\salSkyCorr$), the learned head
only $\galDust\%$ (coefficient of variation of cluster mass $\galCV$ vs.\ $\salCV$). Intra-normalisation removes the difference in cluster mass exactly (\cref{prop:colinv}); the
difference in dust-bin mass survives only as a token re-weighting, which here does not matter.

The dust-bin shows where the heuristic behind (P1) fails. At $\lambda=0$ it changes only the token
weights $w_i$ slightly and is irrelevant ($\famFrNDvsDMean$ points without it, CI
$[\famFrNDvsDLo,\famFrNDvsDHi]$). At $\lambda=1$ it is essential ($\famFrSalNDvsDMean$, CI
$[\famFrSalNDvsDLo,\famFrSalNDvsDHi]$): without a place to put surplus mass, the balanced constraint
pushes abundant tokens, sky included, into clusters they barely resemble; in \eqref{eq:colinv} it
raises exactly their weights $w_i$. Residual NetVLAD aggregation is slightly
below the OT heads on frozen tokens ($\famFrNv\%$).

\subsection{The assignment family with a fine-tuned backbone}\label{sec:ft}
Fine-tuning the last four backbone blocks together with the head (\cref{tab:main}, bottom) improves
every head substantially: GeM by $\ftGemGain$, NetVLAD by $\ftNvGain$, SALAD by $\ftSalGain$ and the
Galatea head by $\ftGalGain$ points R@1 (CI $[\ftGainLo,\ftGainHi]$; hard conditions:
$\galHard\%\to\ftGalHard\%$). Within the OT family the spread stays small (\cref{tab:family},
right): the fixed values of $\lambda$ lie within $\famFtLamRange$ points of each other, and the dust-bin changes R@1 by about one point at most
($\lambda=0$: $\famFtNDvsDMean$ $[\famFtNDvsDLo,\famFtNDvsDHi]$; $\lambda=1$:
$\famFtSalNDvsDMean$ $[\famFtSalNDvsDLo,\famFtSalNDvsDHi]$), presumably because the backbone learns to
make uninformative tokens harmless. The learned head is the weakest member ($\famFtGal\%$;
$\famFtGalVsSalMean$, CI $[\famFtGalVsSalLo,\famFtGalVsSalHi]$ vs.\ SALAD), but the control with
$\lambda$ fixed at its initial value $0.5$ shows that learning is not the cause: $\lambda$ again stays
near its initialisation ($\bar\lambda=\ftGalLam$), and the learned head matches the fixed one
($\famFtLamH\%$; $\famFtGalVsHalfMean$, CI $[\famFtGalVsHalfLo,\famFtGalVsHalfHi]$), as it does on a
frozen backbone ($\famFrGalVsHalfMean$, CI $[\famFrGalVsHalfLo,\famFrGalVsHalfHi]$). Learning $\lambda$
therefore neither helps nor hurts: $\lambda$ stays at a value that, with a fine-tuned backbone, sits at
the low end of a narrow range, a dip that three seeds cannot distinguish from noise.

The largest difference is not inside the OT family at all. NetVLAD, which aggregates residuals to
$k$-means-initialised centroids, becomes the best head ($\ftNvRone\pm\ftNvRonesd\%$). Its closest
OT relative, $\lambda=0$ without a dust-bin, uses the same softmax assignment and no dust-bin, yet
falls behind (NetVLAD minus OT: $\famFtNvVsNDMean$ points, CI $[\famFtNvVsNDLo,\famFtNvVsNDHi]$).
NetVLAD, however, sums residuals of $\ell_2$-normalised reduced features to centroids and initialises
centroids and assignment scores by $k$-means, whereas the OT heads sum the features themselves and
score tokens with a randomly initialised MLP. With trainable tokens this \emph{aggregation} design, not the assignment rule, is
therefore the axis that matters; on frozen tokens the same comparison favours
the OT head ($\famFrNvVsNDMean$ points for NetVLAD).
