We ran two station-level tests of global retrieval on Apollo Hasselblad surface
photographs~\cite{asuapollo} (\cref{tab:real}; protocols in the supplement). \emph{Panoramas}: the
Apollo Lunar Surface Journal~\cite{alsj} lists the frames of each station pan. We query each
frame of $79$ pans ($47$ stations) against the other pans of its mission, which differ in photographer,
standpoint, heading and usually Sun elevation ($\reNq$ queries at $\reNst$ stations of Apollo~15--17). \emph{Rendered map}: for Apollo~15 we rendered $12$ headings at each of $\rrNst$ stations
from the $2$\,m LROC NAC elevation model~\cite{robinson2010lroc}, with the Sun as it stood at each station,
made the views photographic with DESPINA~\cite{nelsonarcher2026despina}, and kept shaded renders as a
control; $\rrNq$ frames from $\rrNpans$ pans are the queries.

On the panoramas no lunar-trained head is clearly better than the zero-shot DINOv2-SALAD ($\reZs\%$, 95\% CI
$[\reZsLo,\reZsHi]$). The frozen cluster-based heads score $\reGal$--$\reSal\%$, and the fine-tuned OT head
differs from the zero-shot model by $\reFtGalVsZsMean$ points ($[\reFtGalVsZsLo,\reFtGalVsZsHi]$), so the
fine-tuning gain seen on LunarSim does not appear. GeM is again the weakest head. Against the
rendered map the zero-shot model is at chance, and only the fine-tuned heads are above it (NetVLAD
$\rrFtNvD\%$, $[\rrFtNvDLo,\rrFtNvDHi]$). DESPINA views raise fine-tuned NetVLAD by $\rrFtNvDmS$ points over
shaded renders, but the interval $[\rrFtNvDmSLo,\rrFtNvDmSHi]$ includes zero. Station positions from 1971 navigation, nearby ground filling
most frames and one map position per station make this test hard.
