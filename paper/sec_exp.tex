\subsection{Terrestrial models under lunar illumination change}\label{sec:zeroshot}
\Cref{tab:main} (top) shows that all three terrestrial models perform reasonably when only the
viewpoint changes (C0: $\zsEigCzero$--$\zsCosCzero\%$ R@1) and degrade sharply once the Sun moves.
DINOv2-SALAD, the strongest of them, falls from $\zsSaladCzero\%$ at C0 to
$\zsSaladCtwo\%$ under grazing illumination (C2) and to $\zsSaladCfive\%$ when the shadows flip (C5).
The CNN-based CosPlace and EigenPlaces fare worse, with C5 values of $\zsCosCfive\%$ and $\zsEigCfive\%$.
Even a foundation-model backbone pre-trained on 142M images therefore does not provide
illumination-invariant place descriptors on regolith. Local re-ranking does not close the gap
either. Fusing MNN+MAGSAC++ matches into the top-10 zero-shot SALAD candidates (selected on
validation as in \cref{sec:rerank}) raises R@1 to $\zsRRRone\%$. That is still below every
lunar-trained cluster-based head \emph{without} re-ranking, and only $\zsRRHard\%$ on the hardest
conditions, where for $\zsHardMiss\%$ of the queries the correct place is not among the ten candidates
at all.

\subsection{Global--local fusion}\label{sec:rerank}
\Cref{tab:rerank} re-ranks the top-10 candidates of the frozen reference head (seed~0, R@1
$\rrGlobRone\%$) with five local scorers. Two findings stand out. First, ranking by the local score
alone barely helps and can hurt: no image-plane scorer improves R@1 by more than about three points, and several
lose ground. Second, fusing local and global evidence with \cref{eq:fusion} helps every scorer. With
partial FGW, R@1 rises to $\rrFgwRone\%$, a gain of $\rrGainMean$ points (95\% CI
$[\rrGainLo,\rrGainHi]$), and on the hardest conditions from $\galSzeroHard\%$ to $\rrFgwHard\%$. Among the first four scorers, differences in the fused setting are small and
within the confidence intervals: FGW vs.\ LightGlue $\fgwVsLgMean$ $[\fgwVsLgLo,\fgwVsLgHi]$, and FGW
vs.\ appearance-only OT $\fgwVsUotMean$ $[\fgwVsUotLo,\fgwVsUotHi]$. The training-free structural
matcher thus performs as well as a learned terrestrial matcher. In the fused setting, MAGSAC++ helps
MNN and LightGlue but not the OT matchers, presumably because their dust-bins already reject most
outliers. Fused, CLIPPER with MAGSAC++ is the strongest scorer ($\clTwoRRone\%$), $\clTwoRVsFgwMean$ points
above FGW ($[\clTwoRVsFgwLo,\clTwoRVsFgwHi]$) and $\clTwoRVsMnnRMean$ above MNN+MAGSAC++
($[\clTwoRVsMnnRLo,\clTwoRVsMnnRHi]$). Exact 3-D keypoint positions ($\dagger$) help neither structural matcher, since
only about $\diagCorrFrac\%$ of the keypoints of a query and its place have a true partner (supplement).

\paragraph*{Why local-only re-ranking fails and fusion works}
We split the effect of re-ranking (supplement) into queries it \emph{rescues} (wrong global top-1,
correct after re-ranking) and queries it \emph{breaks} (the reverse). Local-only ranking rescues many
queries, $\rbFrFgwLocRes\%$ with FGW, but breaks as many ($\rbFrFgwLocBrk\%$): SuperPoint responses on
regolith are dominated by shadow edges, so a wrong candidate photographed under similar light can
collect more matches than the right one. The overrides that break a correct result are those against
a confident global ranking: the median global similarity margin they overturn is $\marginBroken$,
twice that of the overrides that rescue a query ($\marginRescued$). Under fusion, overturning a
larger margin requires proportionally more local evidence (\cref{prop:override}(i)); the hard limit
(ii) binds for fewer than $0.1\%$ of the queries with the frozen head (supplement). Fusion keeps about half of the rescues ($\rbFrFgwFusRes\%$) while
breaking only $\rbFrFgwFusBrk\%$, and the same pattern holds for every scorer and for the fine-tuned
stage~1, where local-only ranking breaks up to $\rbFtLgLocBrk\%$ of the queries and fusion at most
$\rbFtMnnRFusBrk\%$.

\paragraph*{Robustness across stage-1 models}
Repeating the protocol on all three seeds of each stage-1 model, and re-tuning $\beta,\kappa$ on
validation each time, fusion with FGW adds $\msFgwGain\pm\msFgwGainsd$ points to the frozen reference
head (MNN+MAGSAC++: $\msMnnRGain\pm\msMnnRGainsd$; plain MNN: $\msMnnGain\pm\msMnnGainsd$),
$\ftFgwGain\pm\ftFgwGainsd$ to the fine-tuned reference head (MNN+MAGSAC++: $\ftMnnRGain\pm\ftMnnRGainsd$)
and $\msnvFgwGain\pm\msnvFgwGainsd$ to the fine-tuned NetVLAD head (MNN+MAGSAC++:
$\msnvMnnRGain\pm\msnvMnnRGainsd$). The gain shrinks as stage~1 improves, since fewer queries remain
to be rescued, and stays positive for every model. The complete system, fine-tuned NetVLAD with FGW fusion, reaches
$\msnvFgwRone\pm\msnvFgwRonesd\%$ R@1 and $\msnvFgwRfive\%$ R@5 ($\msnvFgwHard\%$ on the hardest
conditions), against $\zsSaladRone\%$ for the best terrestrial model. Under fusion the local scorers differ by at most a few
points, a smaller effect than fusion itself.
