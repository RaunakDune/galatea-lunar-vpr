\subsection{Terrestrial models under lunar illumination change}\label{sec:zeroshot}
\Cref{tab:main} (top) shows that all three terrestrial models perform reasonably when only the
viewpoint changes: at C0, R@1 is $\zsEigCzero$--$\zsCosCzero\%$. They degrade sharply as soon as the
Sun moves. DINOv2-SALAD, the strongest of them, falls from $\zsSaladCzero\%$ at C0 to
$\zsSaladCtwo\%$ under grazing illumination (C2) and to $\zsSaladCfive\%$ when the shadows flip (C5).
CosPlace and EigenPlaces fare worse ($\zsCosCfive\%$ and $\zsEigCfive\%$ at C5): even a foundation-model
backbone does not give illumination-invariant descriptors on regolith. Local re-ranking does not close the gap
either. Fusing MNN+MAGSAC++ matches into the top-10 zero-shot SALAD candidates (selected on
validation as in \cref{sec:rerank}) raises R@1 to $\zsRRRone\%$. That is still below the lunar-trained
stage~1 \emph{without} re-ranking, and only $\zsRRHard\%$ on the hardest conditions, because the
correct place is often not among the candidates at all.

\subsection{Lunar-adapted OT aggregation}\label{sec:stage1}
Training only the aggregation head on illumination-diverse LunarSim scenes (backbone frozen) lifts
R@1 from $\zsSaladRone\%$ to $\galRone\%$ (Galatea, three seeds; paired gain $\gainZsMean$ points, 95\% CI
$[\gainZsLo,\gainZsHi]$), and mean R@1 over the three hardest conditions (C2, C5, C6) from
$\zsSaladHard\%$ to $\galHard\%$. All three cluster-based heads clearly beat every zero-shot model, whereas GeM pooling of the same
frozen tokens does not ($\gemRone\%$): global pooling cannot separate place-specific from
illumination-dependent tokens~\cite{izquierdo2024optimal}.

Galatea and SALAD are statistically indistinguishable: the paired difference in R@1 is
$\galVsSalMean$ points (95\% CI $[\galVsSalLo,\galVsSalHi]$), as \cref{prop:colinv} predicts, although
they spend mass very differently: the balanced head sends $\salDust\%$ of the token mass to the dust-bin
(correlated with the sky fraction, $\rho=\salSkyCorr$), the unbalanced one only $\galDust\%$. The
assignment's marginal constraints are thus not the bottleneck; the training distribution is
(\cref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Joint backbone fine-tuning}\label{sec:ft}
Fine-tuning the last four backbone blocks together with the head (\cref{tab:main}, bottom) improves
every head substantially: GeM by $\ftGemGain$, NetVLAD by $\ftNvGain$, SALAD by $\ftSalGain$ and
Galatea by $\ftGalGain$ points R@1 (CI $[\ftGainLo,\ftGainHi]$; hard conditions: $\galHard\%\to\ftGalHard\%$).
Once the tokens can adapt, the heads are no longer equivalent. NetVLAD becomes the best head ($\ftNvRone\pm\ftNvRonesd\%$; $\ftNvVsSalMean$
$[\ftNvVsSalLo,\ftNvVsSalHi]$ points over SALAD). Within the OT family, which shares the aggregation
architecture, the two end points perform alike ($\lambda=1$: $\ftSalRone\%$; $\lambda=0$:
$\ftLamZeroRone\pm\ftLamZeroRonesd\%$), while learned $\lambda$, which again stays near its initialisation
($\bar\lambda=\ftGalLam$), is slightly worse ($\ftGalVsSalMean$ $[\ftGalVsSalLo,\ftGalVsSalHi]$ vs.\ SALAD).
\Cref{prop:colinv} explains why $\lambda$ carries little signal, not which value is best once the backbone
moves. NetVLAD and the $\lambda=0$ OT head both use a softmax assignment, so NetVLAD's lead must come from
its residual aggregation, $k$-means initialisation or lack of a dust-bin, not from $\lambda$. In practice, an end point $\lambda\in\{0,1\}$ is
preferable to a learned intermediate value.

\subsection{Global--local fusion with OT re-ranking}\label{sec:rerank}
\Cref{tab:rerank} re-ranks the top-10 candidates of Galatea (seed 0, R@1 $\rrGlobRone\%$).
Two findings stand out. First, ranking by the local score alone barely helps and can hurt: no scorer
improves R@1 by more than about two points, and several lose ground. The reason is that SuperPoint
responses on regolith are dominated by shadow edges, which move with the Sun. Second, fusing local
and global evidence with \cref{eq:fusion} helps every scorer. With partial FGW, R@1 rises to
$\rrFgwRone\%$, a gain of $\rrGainMean$ points (95\% CI $[\rrGainLo,\rrGainHi]$), and on the hardest
conditions from $\galSzeroHard\%$ to $\rrFgwHard\%$, the best of all scorers on that subset.
Differences between scorers in the fused setting are small and within the confidence intervals:
FGW vs.\ LightGlue $\fgwVsLgMean$ $[\fgwVsLgLo,\fgwVsLgHi]$, and FGW vs.\ appearance-only OT
$\fgwVsUotMean$ $[\fgwVsUotLo,\fgwVsUotHi]$. The training-free structural matcher thus performs as
well as a learned terrestrial matcher. MAGSAC++ helps MNN and LightGlue but not the OT matchers, whose dust-bins already reject outliers.
The effect is robust across stage-1 seeds. Repeating the protocol on all three Galatea seeds
(re-tuning $\beta,\kappa$ on validation each time), fusion gains $\msFgwGain\pm\msFgwGainsd$ points R@1
with FGW, $\msMnnRGain\pm\msMnnRGainsd$ with MNN+MAGSAC++ and $\msMnnGain\pm\msMnnGainsd$ with plain MNN.
On the fine-tuned Galatea heads the fusion gain is smaller but still consistent:
$\ftFgwGain\pm\ftFgwGainsd$ points with FGW and $\ftMnnRGain\pm\ftMnnRGainsd$ with MNN+MAGSAC++ (R@1
$\ftFgwRone\%$), and fusing FGW into the fine-tuned NetVLAD (seed 0) gives the best overall R@1 of
$\ftNvFgwRone\%$ ($\ftNvFgwHard\%$ on the hardest conditions). The practical lesson is that, on the
Moon, \emph{how local and global evidence are combined matters more than which local matcher is used}.
